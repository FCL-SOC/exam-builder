## Lesson plans: the LEARN framework

A lesson plan has five sections. Write each one as described here, short and specific to this lesson.

## L: Learning Clarity

Three bold headings, in this order, and nothing else:

**Learning Intentions**
One sentence starting with "To", using a Bloom's verb (for example: To explain how enzymes speed up reactions.)
**Success Criteria**
Up to three lines starting with "I can", building from an accessible one (identify, recall) to a stretching one
(apply, evaluate). Only as many as the lesson needs; don't pad to three.
**Do Now**
One sentence: a short retrieval task students do as they arrive, on the previous lesson's content (or prerequisite
knowledge if you don't know what came before).

## E: Explain

About five lines at most. Each key term as a bold heading, followed by one short definition sentence (under 15 words,
key word in *italics*). Then one short worked example under a bold **Example** heading: formula, substitution,
answer, on at most three lines. No introduction sentence.

## A: Application

Two or three lines that direct students to practice, usually from their textbook, split by difficulty, under a bold
activity title. For example: **Practice** Complete Exercise 6I: Q1–6 (guided), Q7–10 (mixed). Extension: worded
problems. Use the teacher's exercise if they gave one; if you don't know it, write the structure and ask the teacher
for the exercise number. Only write original questions if the teacher asks.

## R: Reflect

Exactly two bullet points, one sentence each, no headings:
- a short reflection activity that links back to the success criteria
- a quick exit-ticket question that shows whether the learning intention was met

## N: Next Steps

One sentence naming what the next lesson covers. No headings, bullets or links.

## Formatting (this is what Compass and Word understand)

- Only these: **bold**, *italic*, a line starting with "- " for a bullet, "1. " for a numbered line, and maths.
  No headings with #, no tables, no indented lists.
- Maths always goes inside $ signs: $x^2$, $3x + 5$. Never write \frac, \sqrt or ^{ } outside them, and never use
  \( \) or \[ \].
- Anything with a fraction, root, sum or integral goes on its own line inside $$ … $$, opening and closing on that
  same line, with nothing else on the line.
- Ordinary words and simple numbers stay plain: not $20$ minutes.
- One short expression per equation: a single formula or a single substitution step. Never chain several steps on
  one line (Area = ½ab sin C = ½ × 5 × 7 × sin 30° = 8.75): it overflows the Compass table. Put each step on its own
  line.
- Money in text is written \$12.50 (backslash, dollar); a bare $ starts maths.
